Google reviews for salons, clinics, garages and services

In an appointment-based business, you know your customers and you have time with them. That’s a huge advantage for reviews — as long as you ask at the right moment and follow a few rules specific to your field.

In this guide

  1. The right moment: at the end, not during
  2. Where to place the card
  3. Sending the link by text or email?
  4. Clinics and regulated professions: care in your replies
  5. Google’s rules, for everyone

The right moment: at the end, not during

  • Hair or beauty salon: when the client sees themselves in the mirror, and at checkout.
  • Clinic: at reception, when booking the next appointment or paying.
  • Garage: when handing back the keys, once the work has been explained.
  • Studio, gym, classes: at the end of a session, not the start.

Where to place the card

At reception, next to the payment terminal: every customer passes through there. A second card in the waiting room works well too: people have their phone in hand and time to spare.

Sending the link by text or email?

You can, but two laws apply:

  • Canada’s Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) governs commercial electronic messages. Make sure you have the customer’s consent to write to them, and offer an easy way to stop receiving messages.
  • Québec’s Law 25 governs the use of personal information: only use contact details for the purposes the customer gave them for.

A card at the counter avoids these questions: the customer makes the gesture themselves, without you having to write to them.

Clinics and regulated professions: care in your replies

When you reply to a review, never confirm that someone is your patient or client, and give no detail about any treatment or file — even if the person mentions it themselves. A general reply (“Thank you for your message; please call us to discuss it”) protects confidentiality. Also check your professional order’s rules on advertising and testimonials.

Google’s rules, for everyone

  • No reward for a review (discount on the next appointment, free product).
  • Ask every customer, not only the ones who seem happy.
  • No reviews written by staff, family or accounts created for the occasion.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask a regular customer for a review?+

Yes, any customer. A loyal customer who has never left a review is often the happiest to do it: you just have to ask.

What if a customer leaves an unfair negative review?+

Reply calmly and in general terms, with no personal detail. If it breaks Google’s rules (fake customer, hate speech), flag it from your profile.
