How to get more Google reviews: a guide for local businesses

Your customers are happy, but your Google profile doesn’t show it yet? That’s normal: most people don’t think of it on their own. Here’s how to turn happy customers into reviews, step by step — without breaking Google’s rules.

In this guide

  2. The golden rule: ask — and ask everyone
  3. Seven habits that make the difference
  4. The line that works

Why reviews matter so much

When someone searches “coffee near me” or “mechanic in Laval”, Google shows a map with a few businesses, their rating and their number of reviews. That’s often where the choice is made, before anyone visits a website.

Google says so itself in its help on local ranking: review count and score are among the signals that influence a business’s position in local results. More recent reviews means more visibility and more trust.

The golden rule: ask — and ask everyone

A happy customer rarely leaves a review unprompted. Not for lack of goodwill: they simply don’t think of it, or don’t know where to tap. The fix is simple, but it has to happen at every visit: ask.

And ask everyone. Google forbids asking only the customers you think are happy. It also works better over time: a profile with many varied reviews earns more trust than a perfect one with ten.

Seven habits that make the difference

  1. A direct “Write a review” link. In your Google Business Profile, the “Ask for reviews” option gives you a link that opens the review window directly. Every click you remove is one more customer who follows through.
  2. The right moment. At the end of the visit: at checkout, when handing back the keys, while bagging the order. The experience is still fresh.
  3. One short, sincere line. No speech: one line, always the same, that the whole team knows (see below).
  4. One gesture for the customer. The simpler, the better. An NFC card on the counter opens the review page when the phone comes close: no app, no link to type.
  5. The whole team on board. A thirty-second reminder at the start of each shift is enough. A card nobody mentions works far less.
  6. Reply to reviews. Thank the positive ones, answer the negative ones calmly. Google recommends replying: it shows you read them, and customers who see it are more inclined to write their own.
  7. Track your numbers. How many customers opened the review page this week? Which day, what time? Without measuring, you can’t tell whether a change helps.

The line that works

Short, sincere, and it tells people how little effort it takes:

“If you enjoyed it, a quick Google review helps us a ton — it takes ten seconds!”

Add the gesture: show the card or the link. And reassure: “One sentence is plenty.” What often holds people back is the fear of having to write an essay.

What Google forbids (and so does the law)

  • Offering a reward for a review: discounts, a free coffee, a draw. Google forbids it, even if the review could be negative.
  • Asking only happy customers (known as review gating) or discouraging negative reviews.
  • Writing fake reviews, buying them, or having family and staff write them. In Canada, fake reviews can also breach the Competition Act.

Reviews that break these rules can be removed by Google, and the profile can be penalized. The good news: the honest method — asking everyone at the right moment — is also the one that works best.

What about negative reviews?

They’re part of the game, and a 4.7 with a few critiques often looks more credible than a perfect 5.0. Reply quickly and politely, don’t argue in public, and offer to sort things out privately. If a review breaks Google’s rules (hate speech, fake customer, conflict of interest), you can flag it from your profile.

Frequently asked questions

How long before a review shows up?+

Usually quickly, but Google may delay or filter some reviews, for instance when many arrive at once from the same place. One more reason to aim for a steady pace rather than a one-day push.

Can I get a bad review removed?+

Only if it breaks Google’s rules: then you can flag it from your profile. A review that is simply negative stays up; the best answer is a calm, helpful public reply.

How many reviews should I aim for?+

There’s no magic number. Compare yourself with similar businesses nearby, and aim above all for consistency: a few reviews every week beat one burst followed by months of silence.
